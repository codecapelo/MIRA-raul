presentation, laboratory findings, and MRI. The patient was promptly
referred to the neurosurgery department for emergency surgical inter­
vention. Following preoperative evaluations, the patient underwent
laminotomy and hematoma evacuation under general anesthesia. A

hematoma evacuation, meticulous hemostasis was achieved using
techniques such as bipolar cautery and absorbable hemostatic agents.

                                                                                 3. Discussion

                                                                                     The case presented illustrates the complexities surrounding the
                                                                                 diagnosis and management of epidural hematoma resulting in spinal
                                                                                 cord compression. The mechanism in such cases often involves the
                                                                                 sudden accumulation of blood in the epidural space, which exerts
                                                                                 pressure on the spinal cord. This pressure can lead to both immediate
                                                                                 and secondary injuries to neural tissues, including damage to the
                                                                                 vascular endothelium and surrounding soft tissues. These tissues may
                                                                                 include the spinal cord, dura mater, blood vessels, ligaments, and other
                                                                                 components of the epidural space. The pressure exerted by the hema­
                                                                                 toma on these tissues can lead to neural and vascular damage, influ­
                                                                                 encing both immediate symptoms and long-term recovery outcomes [9].
                                                                                 The impact of epidural hematoma on spinal cord structures is profound.
                                                                                 The immediate mechanical compression of the spinal cord due to the
                                                                                 hematoma can lead to both primary and secondary injury mechanisms.
                                                                                 Primary injury occurs due to direct compression of neural tissue, which
                                                                                 can disrupt neuronal function and lead to immediate neurological def­
                                                                                 icits. Secondary injury mechanisms are also critical; they include
                                                                                 ischemia resulting from compromised blood flow, inflammation, and the
                                                                                 release of neurotoxic substances, which can exacerbate neuronal dam­
                                                                                 age [1,19]. In this case, the rapid onset of neurological deficits neces­
                                                                                 sitated prompt imaging and surgical intervention to alleviate the
                                                                                 compression. Spinal cord hematomas are rare conditions classified
                                                                                 based on the location of bleeding: intramedullary, subarachnoid, sub­

                                                                                 rare and typically occurs in cases with minimal initial symptoms [13]. In
                                                                                 our case, the decision to proceed with surgical intervention was sup­
                                                                                 ported by the presence of significant neurological deficits, aligning with
                                                                                 the recommendations in the literature that advocate for surgical man­
                                                                                 agement in symptomatic patients [4,6]. Preoperative neurological status
                                                                                 significantly influences outcomes, with incomplete deficits correlating
                                                                                 to better recovery and shorter hospital stays Factors contributing to poor
                                                                                 postoperative recovery include coagulopathy, larger hematoma size,
                                                                                 and complete preoperative spinal dysfunction [15].
                                                                                     While surgery is generally recommended, conservative management
                                                                                 may be considered for patients without neurological deficits [16]. In this
                                                                                 case, due to neurological deficit, urgent surgery was recommended after
                                                                                 diagnosis through history, examination and radiology. This case high­
                                                                                 lights several important clinical insights. First, it emphasizes the need to
                                                                                 consider SHE in the differential diagnosis for acute neurological deficits
                                                                                 resembling ischemic stroke, particularly in young patients without
                                                                                 traditional risk factors such as trauma or anticoagulation therapy.
                                                                                 Misdiagnosis of SEH as stroke can lead to inappropriate management,
                                                                                 including the administration of thrombolytics, which may worsen the
                                                                                 hematoma and complicate subsequent surgical intervention. Second, the
                                                                                 case underscores the critical role of MRI in differentiating SEH from
                                                                                 other neurological conditions, facilitating timely and accurate diagnosis.
                                                                                 Third, it illustrates the importance of early surgical decompression,
                                                                                 which is pivotal in achieving favorable neurological outcomes. Finally,
                                                                                 this case serves as a valuable teaching tool for emergency and neurology
                                                                                 teams, reinforcing the importance of maintaining high clinical suspicion
                                                                                 for rare conditions like SEH, even in the absence of apparent predis­
                                                                                 posing factors.
